About

Emma is a biologist, artist, and science advocate. As a Ph.D. student at Cornell University, she conducts research on the cell biology of symbiosis in fungi and bacteria. She is broadly interested in how beneficial biotic interactions between diverse organisms shape ecosystems and drive evolution.

This year, she co-led the McClintock Letters initiative, a nationwide op-ed writing campaign that allowed people to hear directly from scientists in their own communities about what it is they do and why it matters. She worked on this and other science advocacy initiatives with the Scientist Network for Advancing Policy (SNAP), of which she is a founding member.

She believes science communication in biology can serve as a powerful tool to not only drive civic engagement and inform policy, but to reinforce our shared humanity by stoking our inherent wonder and appreciation for the natural world. Outside of work, she moonlights as an oil painter and fly fisherman.
